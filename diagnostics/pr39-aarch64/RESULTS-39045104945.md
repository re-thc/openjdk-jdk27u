# Repaired-source native AArch64 verdict

**Targeted native correctness passed; performance qualification failed. PR39
remains draft and is not ready to merge.** The fixed screen found **25
regressions, 10 passes and no inconclusive rows**, with all four admitted
control placements passing. The 97 main rows were not admitted. The remaining
blocker is stable C2 heap-array call/dispatch overhead, including an observed
candidate-only public `Arrays.mismatch` inlining cliff.

[Run 39045104945](https://github.com/re-thc/openjdk-jdk27u/actions/runs/39045104945)
ran once at qualification commit `b394c38d0065ed5e2703cc26e6c217477699bb97`,
attempt 1, after explicit review and launch approval. It completed the full
screen and intentionally failed with `screen gate failed: retain evidence;
no extra samples`. There was no timeout, retry, sample deletion or expansion.

## Fixed screen

Each row ran base/candidate then candidate/base, two fresh forks per image,
three 250 ms warmups and three 250 ms measured iterations. A regression needs
**both >5% and >1 ns/op loss in both orders**. Within-fork spread >15% or
between-fork median spread >10% is inconclusive. Gates were not changed.

The leading 4 KiB native equality row passed the required stable >=5% benefit
in both orders: baseline **301.243 / 298.793 ns**, candidate **138.612 /
133.323 ns**, or **53.99% / 55.38% lower time**. This admitted the rest of the
screen, not the main matrix. All 35 screen rows plus four control placements
completed: **156 forks**. Controls' maximum cross-image deviation was 0.4163%,
maximum endpoint median drift 0.5629%, and maximum within-fork spread 1.3279%.
The large stable heap losses therefore cannot be dismissed as failed controls.

## What recovered and what still blocks merge

Compared with the unchanged historical screen, the three prior native/mixed
prefix regressions now pass, and equal 64 KiB heap moves from +7.90%/+7.50%
to +1.91%/+1.94%, passing its gate. Equal 128 B heap still benefits. These are
independent within-run ratios, not a cross-run absolute speedup claim. The old
eight inconclusive statuses remain historical: six matching cases are stable
regressions here and INT/LONG pass. Two formerly passing large heap prefixes
(25 and 82 bytes) now regress. Historical warmup was two iterations; this
reviewed campaign fixed it at three without changing measured sample counts.

The retained C2 compilation XML and actual byte dumps identify a concrete
remaining direction: preserve caller inlining of public `Arrays.mismatch` and
keep early heap comparisons in that caller. Every affected short BYTE,
BOOLEAN, CHAR, SHORT, FLOAT and DOUBLE worker rejects that public method as
`already compiled into a big method`, then calls separately compiled code;
the baseline inlines its Java word path. INT/LONG retain inlining and pass.
An 8-byte request takes the repaired Java fallback inside the outlined method,
so its 30–42% losses are not evidence of an 8-byte NEON call. Heap 128-byte and
large early differences can return from the guarded scalar prefix before the
stub, yet still pay the outlined call and dispatch. A scalar prefilter alone
did not cure the public-call boundary. For 25/82-byte large heap prefixes,
baseline calls public Arrays with its Java word loop inline, while candidate
inlines public Arrays but calls the compiled
ArraysSupport helper, then enters the 2 KiB bounded stub after two equal words.
Each image already makes a helper call; prefix/cap/stub setup and the changed
call level require scrutiny before selecting any new threshold.

Native/mixed workers call a compiled segment helper in both images. Their
improved rows include public/helper calls, scoped access, chunking and dispatch;
they do not isolate the NEON body. The scalar prefix covers differences
10/25/82, while 1267 lies beyond it. Equal 4 KiB retains its demonstrated
end-to-end benefit. No unmeasured source tuning was added after this screen.

All 156 final normal C2 workers installed during warmup 3 and had no later
worker invalidation. All 468 measured `gc.count` samples are zero; small
profiler allocations remain, so zero allocation is not claimed. Raw bytes,
method/compile IDs, call annotations and logs are retained. Standalone candidate
`ArraysSupport.vectorizedMismatch` fallback traps at BCI 197 occur late in
several console measurement brackets. Selected XML identifies unrelated main
threads for the equal-64-KiB helper traps; those inputs have no 4-byte tail.
These events remain in the evidence, with no broad claim of zero helper
invalidation. Console brackets alone do not locate an event within the timed
worker interval. The NEON runtime stub body and one native baseline bulk-helper
body were not printed, and hsdis was absent. Causal conclusions concern the
observed call paths, without an isolated instruction-cost claim.
